The Gofman Case: What Makes a Head of Mossad

When Netanyahu nominated his military secretary, Roman Gofman, to run the Mossad, MESS used the news to ask what makes a good Mossad chief. Yossi answers from experience, with first-hand stories about two of the chiefs he worked for.

Jump to

  • October 1973. On the morning the war began, Yossi walks into his boss’s London apartment and finds the head of the Mossad, Tzvi Zamir, back from a night with an Egyptian agent, phoning Prime Minister Golda Meir. What Zamir says next shows that even he didn’t grasp what was coming.

  • 1978. Yossi, then the Mossad’s Iran analyst, briefs members of the Knesset. When he compares Iran’s Islamists to Israel’s religious parties, the head of the Mossad stops him: no allusions to Israeli politics. The lesson brings the episode back to Gofman, who comes to the job from Netanyahu’s office.

Ep. 10 · March 28, 2026 · 29 min

A Heart Attack, Missile Alerts, and the Iran War

Recorded four weeks into the war with Iran, days after Yossi’s heart attack. The war had closed their studio at the Tel Aviv public library, so they talked online from home. Halfway through, a real missile alert cuts Daniella off mid-question. They stop until it’s over, then come back and pick up the same question. It sounds like life in Israel this year, not like a studio.

Jump to

  • The war. Daniella, who grew up with Egypt and Syria as the enemies, asks why Iran is doing this: “Why is it happening? Make it stop.”

  • Before 1979. When Israel and Iran were allies.

  • The alert. A real missile alert cuts the conversation off.

Ep. 20 · August 28, 2026 · 26 min

Not My Zionism

Listeners send in questions, and some become episodes. This one came from New York: how did Yossi become a Zionist, and what does he make of the word now? His answer starts in 1950s Washington, with a Jewish education in which the Holocaust took up two sentences, and turns on the Eichmann trial. Then Daniella brings him to the present and pushes back as he explains why the Zionism winning in Israel today is “not my Zionism.”

Jump to

  • 1961. A Columbia student of Chinese, alienated from his Jewish surroundings, follows the Eichmann trial, which Ben-Gurion meant as a wake-up call. “He was targeting me… And I bit.”

  • From October 7 back to 1967. What Yossi thinks went wrong, and why he calls it “not my Zionism.”

The hosts

The analyst

Yossi Alpher

Served in the Israel Defense Forces as an intelligence officer and in the Mossad in operational and analytical roles, and later directed the Jaffee Center for Strategic Studies at Tel Aviv University. His books include Periphery: Israel’s Search for Middle East Allies and the prize-winning Winners and Losers in the ‘Arab Spring’.

The interviewer

Daniella Alpher

Formerly a television producer at ABC News in New York, making news segments for Good Morning America. Now a tech executive in Tel Aviv.
